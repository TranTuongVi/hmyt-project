### B3. Comparison with the literature
Comparison of Section B1 results against 2–3 papers in Section A2:
1. **Comparison with Kalpna Guleria et al. (2022) [Paper #7 in A2 / P005 in Slide 28]:**
   - Published results of Guleria et al.: Decision Tree achieved Accuracy 99.58%, Random Forest achieved 99.31% on the WEKA/UCI 3,772 records dataset.
   - Proposed model of our team: Random Forest and HistGradientBoosting with Zero-Leakage pipeline achieved Test Balanced Accuracy 99.09% – 99.85%, Test Macro-Recall 99.09% – 99.85%, detecting 100.0% of primary hypothyroidism cases (31/31 cases).
   - **Aspects making the comparison unfair / non-equivalent:** (i) *The Accuracy trap and failure on minority classes:* Guleria et al. optimized conventional Accuracy; in their original paper (Table 1, p. 5), secondary hypothyroidism failed completely across all 5 models (Recall = 0.0%), yet overall Accuracy remained at an artificially inflated 99.58% due to >92% negative prevalence; our team utilizes Balanced Accuracy and Macro-Recall for clinical fidelity; (ii) *Different splitting protocol:* Guleria et al. performed record-level 10-fold CV without auditing duplicate vectors; our team conducted a single, strictly isolated evaluation on the locked benchmark test partition (`allhypo.test`, n = 972); (iii) *Preprocessing transparency:* Guleria et al. did not disclose missing value imputation or handling of typographical outliers (age 455).
2. **Comparison with M. Saleem et al. (2026) [Paper #1 in A2]:**
   - Published results of Saleem et al.: Soft-voting Ensemble (SVM + Random Forest + Gradient Boosting, SMOTE) achieved Accuracy 88%, Macro-F1 0.87, ROC-AUC 0.92 on a private dataset of n = 1,394 records, 14 features, 3 classes (pp. 6–7).
   - Proposed model of our team: Random Forest / HistGradientBoosting with Zero-Leakage pipeline achieved Test Macro-F1 98.19% – 98.71%, Test Balanced Accuracy 99.09% – 99.85% on the official held-out test set n = 972 (Baseline 1 Logistic Regression: Test Macro-F1 86.44%).
   - **Aspects making the comparison unfair / non-equivalent:** (i) *Different datasets:* Saleem et al. utilized private data from a single facility in Srinagar with 14 features, while our team uses the Garvan/Quinlan 1987 benchmark with 27 predictors; (ii) *Different class definitions:* Saleem et al. lacked the subclinical/compensated hypothyroidism class, whereas our team distinguishes `negative` / `compensated` / `primary`, yielding non-comparable Macro-F1 averages; (iii) *Different evaluation protocol:* Saleem et al. utilized a single 80/20 hold-out split with test counts conflicting with class distributions and no external validation; our team utilized 15-fold CV internally and locked test evaluation; (iv) *Higher scores do not signify algorithmic superiority:* Garvan dataset labels are predominantly governed by physiological TSH and FTI cutoffs (incorporation bias, see B5 Risk 3), hence 0.987 vs 0.87 reflects differing intrinsic dataset complexity.
3. **Comparison with S. Seelam (2023) [Paper #10 in A2]:**
   - Published results of Seelam: On the UCI Hypothyroid 3,772 dataset with binary labeling (hypo vs negative) and a test set of 755 samples (697 negative, 58 positive), Decision Tree achieved Accuracy 99.87%, Logistic Regression 98.98%, Gaussian Naive Bayes 45.96% (pp. 40–42).

### B5. Risks / blockers / questions for the instructor
- **Risk 1 — Severe label leakage from procedural measurement flags (`TSH_measured` Leakage):** Through Odds Ratio analysis (Slide 16), `TSH_measured` displays $OR = 69.2$ ($95\%$ CI: $[4.31, 1,111.5]$). When `TSH_measured = f` (physician did not order TSH test), $100\%$ of cases are classified as `negative`. This represents an administrative procedural proxy leakage rather than a pathophysiological signal. Our team resolved this by conducting ablation studies removing measurement flags to evaluate performance strictly on genuine clinical symptoms and laboratory concentrations.
- **Risk 2 — Duplicate records and absence of genuine patient identifiers (Patient-Level Cluster Independence):** The Garvan 1987 dataset provides only incremental integer `record_id`s corresponding to row indices, lacking true Medical Record Numbers (MRNs). Our audit revealed 11 identical clinical feature vectors across the train and test partitions (likely repeated outpatient encounters). Although we strictly adhered to Ross Quinlan's official partition to maintain benchmark comparability, clustering leakage risk remains an inherent limitation of historical registries.
- **Risk 3 — Incorporation Bias & Multicollinearity:** Thyroid diagnostic labels directly incorporate serum TSH (threshold $\approx$ 6 mIU/L) and free thyroxine index FTI (threshold $\approx$ 65 Index), overlapping with the primary predictive features. Furthermore, TT4 and FTI exhibit strong Spearman collinearity ($\rho = 0.74$, VIF > 25) since FTI is mathematically derived ($\text{FTI} \approx \text{TT4} / \text{T4U}$). Our team successfully mitigated this via L2 regularization in Logistic Regression and orthogonal feature splitting in tree ensembles.
- **Questions for the Instructor:** Given that secondary hypothyroidism (central origin) comprises only 2 cases in the training set ($0.07\%$) and 0 cases in the test set ($n = 0$), our team pruned the target space to 3 classes to prevent undefined division-by-zero anomalies in Recall ($0/0$). For the final defense, does the instructor recommend incorporating an auxiliary anomaly detection module (One-Class SVM / Isolation Forest) as a dedicated screening safeguard for suspected central thyroid failure?

- **Matching reporting guideline (TRIPOD+AI / STARD-AI / DECIDE-AI) and why (Appendix §3.2):** The appropriate guideline is **TRIPOD+AI** (Transparent Reporting of a multivariable prediction model of an individual prognosis or diagnosis - Artificial Intelligence extension; Collins et al., 2024).

It does not evaluate the stand-alone diagnostic accuracy of novel medical AI hardware against a reference standard (the scope of STARD-AI), nor is it an active bedside randomized clinical trial intervention (the scope of DECIDE-AI). Therefore, TRIPOD+AI is the sole rigorous standard.
